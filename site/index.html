Certificates are getting shorter. Your spreadsheet is not keeping up.

Browsers and certificate authorities are cutting the maximum lifetime of public certificates. What was a yearly chore becomes a monthly one, and a forgotten certificate or a changed DNS record is an outage or a security hole.

100 days

Maximum certificate lifetime from 2027, down from a year.

47 days

Maximum certificate lifetime from 2029. Manual renewal stops being realistic.

NIS2

Requires demonstrable control, also from companies that are suppliers in someone else's chain.

What it keeps an eye on

Add a domain or an endpoint and the checks start. Every value is stored as a baseline, so a change is noticed, recorded and alerted.

Certificates

  • Found through Certificate Transparency logs and TLS endpoint scans
  • Expiry, issuer and whether renewal looks manual or automated
  • Untrusted, expired and soon-to-expire certificates flagged

DNS

  • SPF, DKIM, DMARC, CAA, MX, NS and DNSSEC
  • Dangling CNAMEs that allow subdomain takeover
  • Any change to a record is a recorded event

Alerts

  • Slack, a generic webhook (PagerDuty, Opsgenie) or email
  • Sent when a status changes or a record drifts
  • Dashboard and change history in English and Dutch

The report is the point

Auditors and customers do not want a dashboard; they want proof. FullyQualified turns the latest results into a NIS2 report as PDF and Excel: your certificate inventory, your DNS controls per domain, and what changed in the last 90 days.

A report is evidence for the technical part of NIS2, not a compliance certificate. NIS2 covers far more than certificates and DNS.

From zero to first report in minutes

The goal is value within ten minutes of starting.

  1. Add your domains

    Type a domain or host:port. Certificates are discovered from CT logs and by scanning.

  2. Read the results

    See what expires, what is untrusted and which DNS controls are missing.

  3. Hand over the report

    Download the NIS2 report as PDF or Excel, and let alerts catch what changes next.

Self-host it in one command

FullyQualified is one .NET container and a Postgres database. Everything in the repository is open source under the AGPL-3.0, including accounts with passkeys, roles and single sign-on through OpenID Connect.

Then open http://localhost:8080 and create the first account. Cloud connectors, starting with Azure, are on the roadmap.

git clone https://github.com/pimvd/fullyqualified
cd fullyqualified
docker compose up

Questions

Does FullyQualified change anything in my environment?

No. It only reads: public DNS queries, TLS handshakes with the endpoints you add, and Certificate Transparency logs. Changing things, such as automated renewal, is a possible later step and would be opt-in.

What does it check?

TLS certificates (expiry, issuer, trust) and DNS records: SPF, DKIM, DMARC, CAA, MX, NS, DNSSEC and dangling CNAMEs. It records every change against a baseline and alerts you through Slack, a webhook or email.

Does the report make me NIS2 compliant?

No. It gives you evidence for the certificate and DNS part of your technical measures. NIS2 compliance is much broader and is your responsibility.

Where does my data live?

When you self-host, on your own server in your own Postgres database. The planned hosted version runs in the EU on Scaleway.

How do people sign in?

With passkeys, so there are no passwords to leak. Single sign-on through OpenID Connect is supported too, and there are three roles: viewer, member and admin. New people join through single-use invitations.

Is it free?

Self-hosting is free and open source under the AGPL-3.0. A paid hosted version is planned; prices are not set yet.
