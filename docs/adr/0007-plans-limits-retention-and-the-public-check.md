# 7. Plans, limits, retention, tenant deletion and the public check

Status: proposed. Builds on ADR 0006.

- **Plans are data.** Four rows in `tiers`: Free, Starter, Business and Custom, each with limits on domains, endpoints and users, whether the NIS2 report is included, and how many months of history are kept. A null number means unlimited. The platform owner edits them on `/limits`; the first values follow `docs/PLAN.md` (domains and history) and are otherwise starting points. A test keeps the plan, the seed in the migration and the code defaults identical.
- **Per-tenant overrides.** A tenant has a tier and optional overrides of each value: blank follows the tier, a number sets it, `unlimited` lifts it (stored as -1). Custom is the plan for agreements per customer and is normally used with overrides. The effective limit is the override, otherwise the tier.
- **The management tenant runs on Custom, unlimited**, so a self-hosted install has no limits unless its owner sets some. A new tenant starts on Free unless the owner picks another plan.
- **Enforcement is server-side, where things are added:** assets (per kind, on the dashboard and during certificate discovery), users (invitations, redemption and SSO sign-up; open invitations count, so a plan cannot be exceeded by inviting first), and the report endpoints. Existing data is never removed because a limit was lowered; only adding is refused.
- **Retention** runs from the scheduler every six hours. It deletes results, changes and alarm log entries older than the period, but keeps the newest result of every check and the newest one that was not an error, so assets never go blank and change detection keeps its baseline. Tenants without a period keep everything.
- **Deleting a tenant** removes every row it owns in one transaction and ends its sessions. A tenant's own admin or the platform owner may do it, both must type the exact name, and the management tenant cannot be deleted. There is no soft delete or undo; backups are the operator's.
- **The public check** (`/scan`) lets anyone check the TLS certificate and DNS of a public domain, as the lead magnet in the plan. Because strangers choose the target, it is off unless `PublicScan:Enabled` is set, and then it takes only public host names on port 443, refuses private and local addresses regardless of other settings, connects to the address it validated (so DNS cannot change its answer between check and connect), limits each client address per hour and the number of concurrent scans, stores nothing, and does not log the domain. The marketing site is plain HTML; its form is a GET to the app's `/scan` and the only thing on the site that leaves it. The app's address on the site is a placeholder until the hosted app has one.
- **Also fixed on the way:** scans of hosts that must be refused now connect to the checked address for assets too, and an unspecified address such as 0.0.0.0 is refused instead of throwing.
- **Not included:** prices and billing, quotas for scan frequency, a soft-delete or export before deletion, and per-user limits.
